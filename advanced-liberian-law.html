About this programme

Move from knowing the rule to defending the analysis.

Each subject pairs two advanced lessons with primary research, a fictional problem, competing arguments and a substantial writing assignment. The 273 delivered questions are distinct across 80 quizzes and examinations.

Prior foundational legal study, an LL.B. or equivalent knowledge is strongly recommended. New to law? Start with the Complete Liberian Law Programme before this advanced course.

Study level and qualification

“Master’s-level” describes the intended depth of research, critical analysis and writing. It does not name a degree awarded by TIH.

The LL.B. and LL.M. are distinct qualifications. The University of Liberia lists the Louis Arthur Grimes School of Law as a professional school. Confirm its current programmes directly with the university. These 26 subjects are the scope requested for this TIH course, not a verified official university curriculum or a claim of affiliation.

Build an advanced legal portfolio.

Six portfolios are placed throughout the subject modules. The final module contains an independent research paper, a professional moot and a comprehensive examination. Deliverables are kept privately and completion is self-recorded against the supplied rubrics; tutor marking is not promised.

1. Public Law and Litigation Research File

A fictional public board cancels a licence and commences proceedings against the provider. Construct a record that separates constitutional review, civil procedure and criminal safeguards.

  • A 1,200-word research memorandum with a narrowly framed question, verified primary authorities and one adverse argument per issue
  • A source-status log, procedural chronology and forum map distinguishing civil and criminal routes
  • Two alternative relief sections explaining scope, authority and factual assumptions

2. Private Rights and Community Land Clinic

A fictional cooperative purchases equipment and proposes a lease over disputed community land. A damaged shipment and overlapping survey create different claims.

  • A contract-risk and tort-liability matrix distinguishing parties, conduct and remedies
  • A land-category, boundary and representation report with a version comparison and participation questions
  • A 1,400-word balanced opinion and a plain-language 400-word explainer preserving unresolved rights

3. Public Procurement and Cross-Border Commerce Dossier

A fictional public tender involves a foreign supplier, a director’s interest and a disputed payment instrument. A later judgment is obtained abroad.

  • A procurement criteria audit using current instruments and relevant transition checks
  • A corporate conflict report and payment-chain diagram identifying each asserted claim
  • A 1,500-word cross-border strategy separating procurement review, contractual remedies and judgment recognition

4. Rights, Family and Evidence Research Portfolio

A fictional family disputes estate administration while a community organisation considers a regional rights complaint. An expert report and privileged email are contested.

  • An estate inventory, administration-authority note and confidential-information handling plan
  • An expert-method review and separate privilege schedule with locally sourced objections
  • A 1,600-word research memorandum with a domestic-remedies chronology and citation audit

5. Employment, Environmental and Tax Compliance Opinion

A fictional training enterprise expands its facility, changes staff arrangements and receives a disputed tax assessment. Residents raise a water-access concern.

  • An employment-status and pay reconciliation with auditable assumptions
  • An environmental authorisation and participation matrix tied to the actual revised design
  • A 1,600-word integrated opinion with a tax objection schedule, lawful options and source-based procedural questions

6. Fiduciary Finance and Intellectual Property Case Study

A fictional education fund finances a startup and licenses course media. Its administrator has an undisclosed business interest and a lender claims the startup’s equipment.

  • A trust-classification and fund-reconciliation report distinguishing authority, loss and gain
  • A current CBL instrument-status table and collateral-priority timeline identifying third-party property
  • A 1,700-word fiduciary-and-IP opinion with a rights-and-licences register and a reasoned counterargument

7. Independent Research Capstone

Choose a narrow Liberian legal question arising from the course. Use published sources and fictional or properly public facts; do not collect private client data or claim to conduct supervised legal practice.

  • A 700-word proposal with question, method, scope, ethics and source plan
  • A 3,500–4,500-word critical research paper using at least twelve checked sources, including relevant primary authorities, and an annotated bibliography
  • A claim-to-source matrix, adverse-authority discussion, limitations statement and 500-word revision reflection

8. Professional Moot and Reasoned Opinion

Use one fictional appellate record about a disputed supply agreement, board authority, community access and evidence. Establish the applicable local review framework; argue both sides before deciding.

  • Two opposing 1,500-word memorials with authorities, record pinpoints and precise requested relief
  • Two eight-minute oral submissions and responses to six bench questions per side; self-record or rehearse privately
  • A 1,500-word reasoned opinion, twenty-six-subject portfolio index and rubric-based self-assessment

A practical route through the course

Your 40-week study plan.

Set aside six hours per week. Follow the subjects in order, keep a source-status log and develop your portfolio work as you study. Open a five-week block to see the suggested allocation and checkpoints.

Use each subject’s eight hours well.

  1. Four hours: study the two video seminars, full notes and assigned legal sources. Longer lectures or readings may take more time.
  2. Three hours: develop the writing assignments, case analysis and any portfolio work due in the block.
  3. One hour: complete practice, assessment and revision, including the midterm in its checkpoint block.

The final 32 hours cover the research capstone, moot, reasoned opinion and final examination. Start longer research tasks early and extend the schedule when needed.

This is a suggested pace, not a deadline or academic credit allocation. Portfolio checkpoints identify work to self-assess against the supplied rubric; they do not submit files or record completion. Common questions.

How long does it take?

Allow about 240 hours: 208 across the 26 subject modules and 32 for the final research-and-advocacy capstone. One route is 40 weeks at six hours per week. These are flexible planning estimates, not academic credits; extended research and revision may take longer.

What must I complete?

Complete all 140 steps in order: 52 taught lessons, 52 practice quizzes, 26 module assessments, the 13-question midterm, eight portfolios and the 52-question programme final. Quizzes use the shared 70% pass threshold. Every final question is new, with two questions from each subject.

Will this award a master’s degree?

No. It awards the TIH course-completion certificate for Advanced Liberian Legal Studies Programme. It is not an LL.M., LL.B., university qualification, professional licence or guarantee of admission or employment.

Is this the same as the existing Law programme?

No. This is a new course with its own enrolment, progress, lesson sequence and certificate. The existing Complete Liberian Law Programme remains available as foundational preparation.

Are projects graded by a tutor?

The player records completion and quiz scores. Research and portfolio work uses supplied self-assessment rubrics and private learner files. Tutor grading, dissertation supervision and automatic file submission are not included unless separately arranged with TIH.

What is the price?

Tuition is free. The one-time registration fee for this advanced programme is US$100. Confirm access through the existing TIH payment and approval process.
