Understand the law. Practise the reasoning.

Study 20 subject areas twice: first through foundations, then through applied legal problems. The programme contains 42 modules, 212 sequential learning steps and 124 quizzes and examinations.

  • Navigate Liberian legal sources, courts and constitutional protections
  • Research primary authorities and write case briefs, memoranda and reasoned opinions
  • Analyse contracts, torts, criminal law, procedure and evidence at foundation and applied levels
  • Study land transactions, family, estates, labour, corporate and commercial issues
  • Apply administrative, procurement, tax, human-rights, international, maritime, environmental and IP concepts
  • Build an eight-project legal portfolio and practise trial and appellate advocacy

A complete TIH course

Each taught lesson includes objectives, definitions, a substantive teaching explanation, a worked local classroom example, primary reading, key terms, review questions, activities and an assignment. Quizzes and examinations use distinct questions.

The final assesses all 20 law subjects. Your portfolio includes case briefs, memoranda, litigation files, due diligence, drafting and moot-court work.

This programme supports legal learning and law-school study. It does not award a law degree or professional practising authority.

Build an eight-project portfolio.

Prepare work throughout the course. Each project includes deliverables, a weighted rubric and a completion checklist. Use fictional or appropriately handled public material.

1. Liberian Case Brief and Research Portfolio

Use one published Supreme Court opinion located through the Judiciary. Produce a case brief and a research log. Do not invent a case citation.

  • A one-page case brief with the issue, procedural history, holding and reasons
  • A five-entry primary-source research log with pinpoint references and status checks
  • A 600-word IRAC analysis of a changed fictional fact and one counterargument

2. Contracts and Land Due-Diligence Portfolio

A fictional cooperative wishes to lease a rural workspace and buy equipment. Land authority and delivery terms are disputed.

  • A land-category and authority checklist with unresolved title questions
  • A lease term sheet and equipment-contract formation timeline
  • A 700-word remedies memo distinguishing assumptions from verified rules

3. Rights, Employment and Evidence Memorandum

A fictional worker is dismissed following an accusation based on a cropped message. There is a disputed medical record.

  • An employment chronology and Decent Work Act applicability analysis
  • An evidence table addressing relevance, authenticity and missing context
  • A 900-word balanced memorandum identifying rights, procedures and remedies to research

4. Moot Court Capstone and Reasoned Opinion

Represent each side in a fictional dispute about a supplier contract, corporate authority and defective performance. Then act as the classroom decision-maker.

  • A 1,200-word memorial with issues, primary authorities, record references and requested relief
  • Two five-minute oral submissions responding to three challenging bench questions each
  • A 900-word reasoned opinion and self-assessment against the rubric

5. Civil and Criminal Litigation File

Use two fictional files: a civil equipment claim and a contested criminal allegation. Keep their burdens, procedures and remedies separate.

  • A civil pleading and provisional-relief outline with supporting evidence
  • A criminal charge-and-disclosure checklist and individual elements chart
  • A 1,200-word comparative procedure memorandum with verified local authorities

6. Commercial, Employment and Regulatory Practice Portfolio

A fictional training enterprise plans to incorporate, employ staff, buy goods and bid for a public contract.

  • A corporate authority and conflict checklist plus a sourced compliance calendar
  • Employment terms, pay-reconciliation and safety checklists
  • A sales risk matrix and current PPCC evaluation-and-accountability note

7. Estates, Community Land and Rights Clinic Simulation

A fictional community seeks guidance concerning disputed inherited property, representation and a proposed development.

  • A simulated interview chronology and confidentiality plan
  • An estates-and-land authority matrix and community-participation issues register
  • A 1,200-word balanced legal memorandum and a plain-language public explainer

8. Professional Moot Court and Final Legal Opinion

Use a fictional appellate record involving a company supply contract, a disputed variation and a conflict of interest. Argue both sides before writing a reasoned opinion.

  • Two 1,200-word appellate submissions with primary authorities and record pinpoints
  • Two seven-minute oral arguments plus answers to five bench questions per side
  • A 1,200-word reasoned opinion, eight-project portfolio index and reflective self-assessment

Plan your study.

Suggested pace: 32 weeks at five hours a week. Spend the first 16 weeks on foundations and the next 16 on applied study. Begin the project deliverables early, revise after each subject group, and allow extra time for longer seminars or research.

Who is this complete course for?

Learners seeking a structured study of Liberian law, prospective and current law students, community educators and professionals. The programme starts with foundations and progresses to applied analysis. It requires careful reading, writing and independent research.

How is the programme connected to Liberian law schools?

Its subject coverage draws on published Liberian law-school areas of study and Liberian primary sources. It is an independent TIH course. It is not an official degree programme, institutional partnership, accredited law qualification or permission to practise law.

What must I complete to earn the certificate?

Complete all 212 steps in order, including 80 taught lessons, their practice quizzes, 40 module assessments, both stage examinations and the applied midterm, all eight portfolio projects, and the 60-question programme final. Quizzes use the existing 70% pass threshold. Portfolio work is self-recorded using the supplied rubrics; tutor marking is not promised unless separately arranged.

What happens to my earlier foundation progress?

The original 106 foundation steps stay in the same positions and their question banks stay unchanged. Completed foundation work remains recorded. The former final is now the foundation examination. It does not complete the expanded programme; the applied stage and new programme final are still required. Existing foundation credentials are not converted into the new credential.

How long does the complete course take?

Plan about 160 hours. One suggested route is 32 weeks at five hours per week, or 16 weeks at ten hours per week. Prepare portfolio work throughout both stages. Longer seminars, legal research and revision can take more time; these are estimates, not completion deadlines.

Are foreign video examples Liberian rules?

Some videos are comparative. Every lesson supplies a local reading or a verified source for the relevant framework. Check current Liberian statutes, regulations, court rules and judgments; do not import foreign deadlines, rates or procedures. Classroom scenarios are fictional and the programme is not personalised legal advice.
